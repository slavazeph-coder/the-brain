How this works and what it does not do

A fine-tuned Qwen language model (the planner) reads the instruction and writes a short motion recipe: poses, holds, oscillations and timing for the head, antennas and body. A 21.8M-parameter flow-matching model turns the recipe into a 25 Hz trajectory, and every frame is pulled back into the Stewart platform's reachable set with the SDK's inverse kinematics.

This page plays the result on the open-source Reachy Mini 3D model. It is kinematic playback, not a physics simulation, and no robot is connected. A downloaded move uses the Reachy Mini SDK's recorded-move format; review it before playing it on hardware.

The released models control Reachy Mini's nine motion channels only. They do not drive humanoid arms, legs, locomotion or grasping; other robot bodies need retargeting, safety limits and likely additional training data. Generation needs a separately connected motion worker. Published speeds (about 0.17 s, 0.29 s and 0.8 s for the 0.8B, 4B and 27B planners) were measured by the author on an RTX PRO 6000 and have not been verified on BrainSNN hardware.
