Inspect the surface

Inspect the original mesh locally. No geometry is smoothed or repaired.

Stripes use the supplied shading normals; the topology check uses triangle face normals and exact coincident positions within each mesh. Boundaries and sharp corners may be intentional. No self-intersection or gap check between separate meshes. A mesh cannot certify NURBS G0/G1/G2/G3 continuity. Inspect analytic surfaces in Rhino with Zebra and EdgeContinuity.

Make a clear walkthrough.

Frame a view, save it, then move and save the next. Preview the move before recording. Your original asset stays unchanged.

No camera views saved.

Open a local asset to author a camera path.

Up to 32 views and 60 seconds. Paths bind to the exact asset hash and display orientation. Coordinates use local asset units, not verified metres. Moves can cross surfaces or unseen areas; no collision test is performed. Video uses the actual local renderer and carries origin and scale labels. Generated or completed appearance is excluded from BIM measurements, occupancy and robot routes.

The viewer and parser run on your device; no third-party renderer is loaded. Up to 100,000 points are displayed, with uniform sampling for larger files. Requires WebGL2. Gaussian assets use base colour only; view-dependent shading is not rendered. GLB must be self-contained, without Draco, Meshopt or KTX compression. Export SPZ / KSPLAT assets as uncompressed PLY or SPLAT first.

A point cloud is not a trained Gaussian splat. Neither establishes metric scale, collision geometry or robot safety. Unobserved surfaces remain unknown.
