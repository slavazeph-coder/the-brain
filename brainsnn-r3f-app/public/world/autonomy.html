World Builder / Autonomy trial

Give it sensors. Drop a crate.
Count what breaks.

A simulated 360° lidar and front and rear depth cameras build the robot's own 4 cm map as it drives. A* plans the route, MPPI follows it, and a crate dropped in its way has to be seen before it can be avoided. Then a paired trial runs the same tasks clean and broken on purpose, and counts contacts, safe stops and replan time, on a reviewed map of a real space.

    What this is, and is not

    • A kinematic unicycle at 10 Hz on a planar map: no physics engine, wheel slip, legged gait or contact dynamics. Humanoid and quadruped are body envelopes only.
    • Sensors are ray casts against the reviewed boxes, set from published field of view, range and noise. Reflectivity, multipath, motion blur and the lidar's scan pattern are not modelled.
    • Ground truth is the reviewed map plus what the trial adds. Anything the map got wrong is wrong here too, which is why the map is reviewed first.
    • Replan times are wall-clock on this device; other devices differ.
    • No real robot, learned policy or safety certification. A simulated pass is a reason to run a physical trial, not a substitute for one.

    How it works

    • Sense: rays from a roof lidar and two depth cameras; returns below 5 cm count as floor, higher ones as obstacles. The robot never reads what it hit.
    • Map: log-odds evidence on a 4 cm grid, with the reviewed map as a prior, and a clearance field for planning.
    • Plan: 8-connected A* with an inflation cost, replanned when its route is blocked.
    • Act: MPPI samples control sequences, rolls each out against the robot's own map and blends them by cost.
    • Score: the true footprint against ground truth: contact, keep-out, closest approach. The source is one open module, autonomy.mjs, with its tests in the repository.
